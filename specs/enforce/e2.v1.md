# E2 — Court-grade audit: capture levels, read index, replay

Status: implemented (v0.0.1). Extends [e1.v1.md](e1.v1.md) §5; Linear milestone
E2 · Court-grade audit log.

## §1 Principles

The JSONL hash chain is the **only source of truth**. It records what an agent
sent and what came back **raw** (§2); everything else here is a view of it
(capture levels), a derived, rebuildable projection of it (index, replay), or
an export of it (evidence packs). `AuditChain.verify()` still needs nothing but
the files.

## §2 Payloads: recorded raw, detachable (`detach.py`); capture levels as a view (`redaction.py`)

**v2 (redactable payloads).** Previously the stored action copy was shaped by
the rule's `audit_level` before it was hashed: `minimal` dropped `params` and
`context.extra`, `standard` scrubbed secret-shaped strings. That made the
record itself lossy — an investigation could never see what a `minimal` rule's
agent asked for, replay skipped those decisions, and no export could recover
what the chain had chosen not to keep. And it could not be relaxed the other
way: the entry hash covered the content, so content could not be removed from
an exported excerpt without breaking the chain. Both are fixed by one rule.

**Detached fields.** Content-bearing fields are named per entry shape — engine
decisions: `payload.action.params`, `payload.action.context.extra`; hub
entries: `args`, `result` — and *detached* when the entry is written:

    "detached":   {<dotted path>: sha256(salt + canonical(value)), ...}   hashed
    "salts":      {<dotted path>: base64(16 random bytes), ...}            NOT hashed
    "unrecorded": [<dotted path>, ...]                                     hashed, only with record_payloads off

The chain hash covers `hashable_body(entry)`: the entry minus `sig`, `key_id`,
`hash`, minus `salts`, minus the value at every detached path. `canonical` is
the chain's lenient canonical JSON (sorted keys, `(",", ":")`, UTF-8,
`default=str`). The value stays inline beside its digest.

- **Verify** (`HashChainWriter.verify`, `pack_verify`): the hash check as
  before, then `detach.check` — every present value must have its salt and
  hash to its digest (a value edited after the fact fails: the hash no longer
  covers it, the digest does); an absent value is *withheld* and counted, and
  must not leave its salt behind. `VerifyResult` reports
  `payloads_verified / payloads_withheld / payloads_unrecorded`. A writer's
  own chain should report 0 withheld; a non-zero count there means content was
  deleted after writing (`unified-mcphub audit verify` warns).
- **Redact** (`detach.redact`): removes the values and `salts`, keeps
  `detached`. The hashed body is unchanged, so links and signatures verify.
- **Why salted.** Many inputs are guessable ("UAI-203", a yes/no flag, a short
  customer-number range). An unsalted digest in a digests-only export would let
  anyone holding it confirm a guess. The salt travels with the content and is
  withheld with it; the customer can later produce any single value with its
  salt and the auditor checks it against the signed entry.
- **Backward compatible.** An entry with no `detached` key hashes exactly as
  before; every existing chain verifies unchanged. Entries written before this
  keep their write-time-shaped content (`redacted: true` where scrubbed); that
  content is inside the hash and cannot be withheld from an export.
- **Strictness.** A strict (engine) chain checks the whole entry for floats
  *before* detaching, so detached values stay under the no-floats rule.

**`audit_level` is now a view.** It is still recorded on every entry and is the
default for *display* (`capture_action` / `scrub`, used by the hub CLI's
`audit show/pair/tail/search`, `--raw` to bypass) and the natural input to an
export decision. `action_digest` is, as before, over the full action. New
decision entries carry `redacted: false` — the field stays in the shape because
legacy entries carry `true` and replay reads it. Approval entries detach
nothing: their only content-like field, `scope`, is inside the control plane's
signed resolution, and withholding it would make the approval unverifiable.

**`record_payloads=False`** (`AuditChain(..., record_payloads=False)`,
`HashChainWriter(..., record_payloads=False)`; hub: `audit.record_payloads`).
The explicit escape hatch for deployments that must not keep content at all:
each value is digested with a salt, then dropped; the salt is kept locally (so
a copy held elsewhere can be proved later — `redact` still strips it on
export), and the hashed `unrecorded` list says, under the signature, that the
content was never stored — distinguishable from "withheld from this copy".
Logged as a WARNING on every start. Not the way to keep content out of an
export: that is `detach.redact` / a digests-only evidence pack.

**Evidence packs** (`evidence_pack.py --payloads include|digests-only`,
default include). digests-only rewrites each excerpt line through
`detach.redact` (re-serialised; verification hashes canonical bytes, so this
changes no hash) and records the mode in `meta.json` (`payloads`,
`legacy_inline_entries`), `manifest.json` and the README. `detach.py` is copied
into `verify/` beside `attest.py`; the verifier reports per chain "N verified
against their digests, M withheld" and FAILS on a present-but-mismatched
payload or a salt left without its content.

**Signing keys in a pack.** A chain's `meta.json` names one `public_key` (with
`signed_from_seq`), or — for a reporter that rotated (`--chain-signing
signing.json`) — a `keys` list of `{public_key, key_id, since_seq}`, each
range checked with its own key. The verifier trusts only the key bytes: every
key id it prints is derived from the key (`attest.key_id`; a stated `key_id`
is ignored), every range needs an integer `since_seq`, no two ranges may start
at the same seq, the newest range's key must be the chain's `public_key` and
the first range must start at `signed_from_seq`; any disagreement FAILS the
chain. The chain line lists the ranges (`<kid> from seq N, …`). The builder
refuses `--chain-key` / `--chain-signed-from` values that contradict the
`signing.json` they accompany.

**Hub approvals recorded before `resolution`.** A hub `received` entry with an
attestation but no `resolution` field is checked against the unscoped kinds
(`allow`, `allow_session`, `deny`; scope null). One verifying: VERIFIED, with
the kind recovered. None: a NOTE ("not re-verifiable (recorded before
resolution was recorded)") that does not fail the pack. A recorded kind whose
signature does not verify FAILS.

## §3 SQLite read index (`index.py`)

`AuditIndex` — engine-local SQLite (technologies.md), zero new dependencies.
`refresh(audit_dir)` is incremental (per-file line cursor in `indexed_files`);
`query()` filters on principal / tool / verb / resource / verdict / rule_id /
kind / source plus a `ts` range, newest first; `locate(seq)` returns (file,
line) to pull the full record from the chain. Deleting the `.db` and
re-refreshing rebuilds it from scratch — it is never authoritative.

## §4 Offline replay (`replay.py`)

`replay(audit_dir, engine) → ReplayReport` re-decides every recorded decision
against a (candidate) policy and reports divergences (verdict OR matched rule):
"what would this policy change have done to last month's traffic?" This is the
technical seed of the Test pillar (X2).

Honesty rules: decisions are recorded raw (§2), so every new entry — `minimal`
included — replays against the params it was decided on. What cannot be
replayed is counted as **skipped**, never silently replayed with the missing
part read as empty: entries whose params/extra are *withheld* (a digests-only
excerpt, or `record_payloads` off — also counted in `withheld`), legacy
`minimal` entries (stored `params: null`), and unparseable ones. Legacy
scrubbed entries replay but each divergence carries a `redacted` flag, since a
condition that read a scrubbed value may diverge for that reason alone.

## §5 Hub migration (integration #1)

The chain mechanics are layered: `HashChainWriter` chains FLAT entries
(lock/rotation/recovery/O_APPEND + prev_hash/hash stamping); `AuditChain` is
the engine's `{kind, seq, ts, payload}` format on top. The MCP hub's two-phase
`AuditLog` now builds on `HashChainWriter` directly (`strict=False` — hub
args/results are pre-existing free-form JSON; see canonical.py; `args` and
`result` detached per §2), keeping its flat entry shape, reader, and CLI intact while gaining tamper evidence,
restart-surviving seq, and `unified-mcphub audit verify`. The hub also stamps
each received entry with the canonical Action digest (`hub.py`). Verification
anchors on the first retained entry, so retention pruning does not break it —
head truncation is indistinguishable from pruning unless the anchor is pinned
out-of-band (C2).

## Signing the chain (UAI-145)

`AuditChain(signer=...)` signs each entry's **hash** with Ed25519. That hash
already covers the payload and, through `prev_hash`, everything written before
it — so one signature per entry authenticates the whole history up to that
point, and no separate per-action signature is needed.

This closes a gap between the claim and the code: `Signer` existed, was fully
unit-tested, and was wired to nothing, so "signed, replayable Actions" named a
library capability rather than a shipped behaviour.

What it buys, precisely. The hash chain alone proves *internal consistency*: an
attacker who controls the writer can rewrite the entire history, recompute
every hash, and `verify()` reports it pristine — that is the honest limit of a
hash chain, and there is a test that demonstrates it. A signature makes that
rewrite require the key as well as write access.

What it does **not** buy: protection from a compromised *live* writer, which
holds the key by definition. That still needs external anchoring (Merkle
checkpoints, UAI-134). The three tiers are worth keeping distinct — internal
consistency (chain), attributable authorship (signature), and independent
existence-at-time-T (anchoring).

Off by default, and `verify()` without a public key behaves exactly as before,
so existing chains and callers are unaffected. Key management is deliberately
the caller's problem (`Signer.from_private_bytes`): OS keyring locally, KMS or
HSM in an enterprise, and the engine should not have opinions about which.

### A chain that gains a key mid-life

`HashChainWriter.verify(public_key=..., signed_from_seq=N)` checks a chain that
ran unsigned and was given a key later — the MCP hub before and after it is
given a signing key. Entries with `seq < N` may be unsigned; from the first
entry at or above `N` every entry must carry a valid signature, and the
requirement latches (a later entry renumbered below `N` does not shed it). At
least one entry at or above `N` must exist, or the result is a failure: a
chain that recorded signing from `N` and now ends before it has lost its signed
tail, the one edit that would leave the prefix rewritable undetected.

The unsigned prefix is still protected: the first signed entry's hash covers
its `prev_hash`, which is the hash of the entry before, and so on back to the
anchor. Rewriting the prefix breaks either a hash or the link into `N`, and
mending the link changes `N`'s hash, which its signature no longer matches.
`N` itself must come from outside the chain — "the first signed entry" is
whatever an attacker who stripped earlier signatures says it is — so the host
records it when it happens (the hub: `signing.json`). `HashChainWriter.signer`
is settable after `start()` for exactly this host shape.

## Anchoring (UAI-134)

The third tier, and the one the other two cannot supply. `checkpoint.py` builds
a Merkle root over a contiguous range of entry hashes, signs it, and carries the
previous checkpoint's root so checkpoints chain. `anchors.py` decides where it
goes, which is a trust question rather than a cryptographic one — each
implementation states what it is independent of.

**What it closes.** An attacker who owns the writer rewrites the history,
recomputes every hash, re-signs every entry, and `verify()` reports it pristine;
there is a test that demonstrates exactly that, and it is the honest limit of a
hash chain. It is not a limit on a root somebody else wrote down last week. A
reissued checkpoint for a covered range with a different root is not ambiguous —
a hash chain cannot produce one.

**Why a tree rather than the head.** Inclusion proofs. An investigator proves
one action was in the log at time T with that entry and about twenty hashes,
rather than disclosing a customer's entire operational history. RFC 6962 domain
separation (`0x00` leaves, `0x01` nodes) because naive Merkle trees have a real
second-preimage attack; odd nodes are promoted rather than duplicated, which is
the CVE-2012-2459 shape.

**What is still not closed.** `ControlPlaneAnchor` is independent of the sidecar
and its host — the attacker the chain and the signature both fail against — and
not of the control plane. A customer needing that anchors somewhere we do not
run, which is what the `Anchor` protocol is for. RFC 3161 and transparency-log
clients are the obvious implementations and are deliberately not defaults: both
are a network dependency plus a trust decision that belongs to the customer.

The exposure is the tail. Entries written since the last checkpoint are chained
and signed but not yet pinned, so that range is what a compromised sidecar can
still rewrite undetected — a stated, bounded gap, shortened by checkpointing
more often, and reported as a coverage gap rather than left to be assumed away.
