# E2 — Court-grade audit: capture levels, read index, replay

Status: implemented (v0.0.1). Extends [e1.v1.md](e1.v1.md) §5; Linear milestone
E2 · Court-grade audit log.

## §1 Principles

The JSONL hash chain is the **only source of truth**. Everything added here is
either a shaping of what enters the chain (capture levels) or a derived,
rebuildable projection of it (index, replay). `AuditChain.verify()` still needs
nothing but the files.

## §2 Capture levels (`redaction.py`)

Ports the hub's secret-shape redaction (ADR-0009) with one structural upgrade:

- `action_digest` is **always** computed over the full action — cryptographic
  proof of exactly what happened, without storing it.
- The **stored** action copy is shaped by the matching rule's `audit_level`:
  `minimal` drops `params` and `context.extra` entirely; `standard` scrubs
  secret-shaped strings (GitHub/AWS/Slack/OpenAI-style tokens, JWTs —
  defense-in-depth, leaky by design); `detailed`/`full` store raw.
- `redacted: true` is recorded only when the stored copy actually differs.
- The hash chain covers the stored (post-capture) entry: redaction is part of
  the tamper-evident record, not a mutation after the fact.

## §3 SQLite read index (`index.py`)

`AuditIndex` — engine-local SQLite (technologies.md), zero new dependencies.
`refresh(audit_dir)` is incremental (per-file line cursor in `indexed_files`);
`query()` filters on principal / tool / verb / resource / verdict / rule_id /
kind / source plus a `ts` range, newest first; `locate(seq)` returns (file,
line) to pull the full record from the chain. Deleting the `.db` and
re-refreshing rebuilds it from scratch — it is never authoritative.

## §4 Offline replay (`replay.py`)

`replay(audit_dir, engine) → ReplayReport` re-decides every recorded decision
against a (candidate) policy and reports divergences (verdict OR matched rule):
"what would this policy change have done to last month's traffic?" This is the
technical seed of the Test pillar (X2).

Honesty rules: `minimal`-capture entries can't be replayed faithfully (no
params) and are counted as **skipped**, never silently replayed; scrubbed
entries replay but each divergence carries a `redacted` flag, since a condition
that read a scrubbed value may diverge for that reason alone.

## §5 Hub migration (integration #1)

The chain mechanics are layered: `HashChainWriter` chains FLAT entries
(lock/rotation/recovery/O_APPEND + prev_hash/hash stamping); `AuditChain` is
the engine's `{kind, seq, ts, payload}` format on top. The MCP hub's two-phase
`AuditLog` now builds on `HashChainWriter` directly (`strict=False` — hub
args/results are pre-existing free-form JSON; see canonical.py), keeping its
flat entry shape, reader, and CLI intact while gaining tamper evidence,
restart-surviving seq, and `unified-mcphub audit verify`. The hub also stamps
each received entry with the canonical Action digest (`hub.py`). Verification
anchors on the first retained entry, so retention pruning does not break it —
head truncation is indistinguishable from pruning unless the anchor is pinned
out-of-band (C2).

## Signing the chain (UAI-145)

`AuditChain(signer=...)` signs each entry's **hash** with Ed25519. That hash
already covers the payload and, through `prev_hash`, everything written before
it — so one signature per entry authenticates the whole history up to that
point, and no separate per-action signature is needed.

This closes a gap between the claim and the code: `Signer` existed, was fully
unit-tested, and was wired to nothing, so "signed, replayable Actions" named a
library capability rather than a shipped behaviour.

What it buys, precisely. The hash chain alone proves *internal consistency*: an
attacker who controls the writer can rewrite the entire history, recompute
every hash, and `verify()` reports it pristine — that is the honest limit of a
hash chain, and there is a test that demonstrates it. A signature makes that
rewrite require the key as well as write access.

What it does **not** buy: protection from a compromised *live* writer, which
holds the key by definition. That still needs external anchoring (Merkle
checkpoints, UAI-134). The three tiers are worth keeping distinct — internal
consistency (chain), attributable authorship (signature), and independent
existence-at-time-T (anchoring).

Off by default, and `verify()` without a public key behaves exactly as before,
so existing chains and callers are unaffected. Key management is deliberately
the caller's problem (`Signer.from_private_bytes`): OS keyring locally, KMS or
HSM in an enterprise, and the engine should not have opinions about which.

## Out of scope (still)

- Merkle checkpoints / external anchoring — when a partner asks for
  third-party timestamping.
- Evidence report generation (EU AI Act / NIST AI RMF / ISO 42001) — C2, in the
  control plane, consuming this index.
